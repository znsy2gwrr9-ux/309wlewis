    Buying as a medical professional?

    Doctors, dentists, veterinarians, and other healthcare professionals can often qualify for special mortgage programs, commonly called doctor loans or physician loans. They are built for people with high earning potential, large student loan balances, and little time to save a big down payment.

    Below is a plain-language look at how these loans work and a few programs to ask about. Eligibility differs by lender, so check that your specific license or degree is covered.

    Programs to ask about

    • CMG Financial MedPro

      MedPro Advantage allows as little as 5% down with no mortgage insurance. MedPro Premier offers 100% financing for eligible professions. Both go up to $2 million and can exclude deferred student loans from your debt-to-income ratio.

      Advantage covers a wider range of medical occupations than Premier.

    • Bank of America Doctor Loan

      3% down up to $850,000, 5% down up to $1 million, 10% down up to $1.5 million, and 15% down up to $2 million.

      Licensed and practicing doctors and dentists, residents and fellows, and other eligible medical professionals.

    • Regions Bank

      A Doctor Loan program for physicians at any stage of their career, plus an Emerging Professionals Program for professionals with up to 7 years in their field.

      Emerging Professionals covers pharmacists, nurse anesthetists, PAs, NPs, veterinarians, chiropractors, optometrists, and podiatrists.

    • FNBO

      0% down up to $850,000, and 5% down up to $1.25 million.

      Veterinarians and many other high-income professions.

    • Old National Bank

      0% down up to $750,000.

      Includes veterinarians.

    • Fulton Mortgage

      Financing up to $3 million.

      Pharmacists, veterinarians, and podiatrists.

    What lenders usually ask for

    • Proof of your license or degree, or of residency or fellowship enrollment
    • An employment contract or offer letter, plus recent pay stubs if you have them
    • Student loan statements, including income-driven repayment details
    • A credit score of roughly 660 to 700 or higher, depending on the program

    Worth knowing before you apply

    • Rates can run slightly higher than a standard mortgage, so compare quotes
    • Some programs limit eligibility to the first 7 to 10 years of practice
    • Lenders do not all operate in every state, so confirm availability where this home is located
    • Financing 100% of a home means a larger balance and more interest over time
